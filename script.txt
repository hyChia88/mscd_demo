Motivation and usage scenario
Building projects are titanic endeavors with sky-high costs. They involve a massive web of stakeholders, and the core problem is simple: everyone is speaking a different language. Developers start the project and track it with spreadsheets. Architects and design teams create the vision using BIM and 2D plans. Construction Managers own the execution. And then there are the subcontractors—the boots on the ground who actually build the pieces.
Ideally, when the project starts running, data flows smoothly. But here is what really happens. Subcontractors—often small teams without the resources for fancy tracking headsets—finish a task, snap a quick photo, scribble some notes on a printed floorplan, and hand a stack of paper forms back to the Construction Managers.
The Construction Managers inherit this mountain of random, unstructured paperwork from various teams. Once a month, they sit down for a massive data-entry session to manually update the central BIM cloud. Because of this lag, Developers checking their dashboards are always looking at the past. They can't catch the true progress, leading to a domino effect of delayed work and scheduling conflicts. Not to mention the real-time issues that get lost in the shuffle! Things like, "Hey, it's raining, we need to cover the materials on site!" or "The delivery is arriving next week, we need the site cleared to proceed!" Plus, this industry has high turnover. When people leave, their handwritten notes become ancient hieroglyphs. What does this short-form mean? Why did the previous manager circle this problem, and where exactly is it?
This leads us to a critical question: Given how powerful generic Vision-Language Models are today, why can't AI be our ultimate project companion?
Imagine an AI Interpreter Layer. Subcontractors submit their messy, unstructured data without worrying about labeling. The AI Interpreter instantly translates this into structured data, reliably mapping it to specific tasks, locations, and statuses. Construction Managers receive automated updates that trace directly back to the BIM system. Developers look at random site images and instantly understand where they are located and what the status is, allowing them to update business plans in real time. Ultimately, we could even use on-site cameras to act as a 24/7 virtual workforce, ensuring the physical site perfectly matches the Digital Twin.
But to be this Interpreter, what capabilities does the AI actually need? It requires three things: First, understanding the project metadata. Second, understanding engineering codes and domain knowledge to judge the physical status. And third, understanding the spatial context to know where that status is happening.
We are picking the hardest, most critical slice of the cake first. Before we can ask how or why, we must ask: WHERE IS IT?
How do we define "where"? First, we establish our Ground Truth. In the AEC industry, we use IFC files to ensure interoperability. This allows us to read the data as an IFC Graph—giving us a highly structured, flawless map of the building. Next, we harness AI. We take the generic power of Vision-Language Models and draw inspiration from Scene Graphs, allowing the AI to understand rich, 3D spatial relationships from flat 2D images. Finally, we use a RAG framework to bridge the gap between our Ground Truth database and the visual input.
At a high level, we are solving the challenge of Multimodal Grounding and Schema Alignment. We are taking informal, unstructured site evidence—like chat logs, photos, and drawings—and forcing it to perfectly align with a strict IFC schema, with zero hallucination and zero information loss.
Tackle the locate and grounding part, and the untraceable data problem vanishes. This also paves the way for the AI to understand the status of the elements later on.
So, how does the engine work? We use a Neuro-Symbolic approach wrapped in an Agent Policy. Multiple messy inputs arrive. The Agent learns the inputs, classifies the action, and calls specific tools to parse the task. We introduce a quick human-in-the-loop moment for the user to confirm the target item. This paired dataset is passed into the Vision-Language Model. The AI acts as the "Neuro" layer, understanding the spatial context and outputting a strictly constrained JSON form. Finally, this structured data runs through our "Symbolic" layer—a deterministic query plan against our project database—to retrieve the exact, hallucination-free result.

The beauty of this system is how it transforms the workflow. Subcontractors can keep submitting unstructured, multi-format reports. They don't have to change how they work. The Interpreter-assisted system automatically fills in the metadata. Construction Managers shift from doing tedious data-entry to simply verifying the AI's work before instantly updating the BIM cloud. Developers can finally read the true progress. And it works in reverse! If a Developer questions an update in the system, they can trace the digital metadata all the way back to the raw, original site photo. Further, architects could use this interpreter to understand where to amend in the 3d model from the on-site image taken from subcons. The AEC interpreter add traceability of the unstructured data and helps the interoperability of data.

Basically, the Interpreter Layer acts as the ultimate translator. By mastering the "WHERE," it ensures every piece of unstructured data is permanently linked to structured metadata, like a specific GUID. It makes everything perfectly traceable, finally unlocking the true power of construction data analysis.
